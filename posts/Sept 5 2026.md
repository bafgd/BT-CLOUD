# Drive has a better solution

## The issue
As stated in the last post, I have been facing many issues with OpenCloud as my solution for Cloud Drive. I wanted to look back at NextCloud to see if I could fix the issues I'd had with it before, and see if it could perform better than OpenCloud.

## The Solution

### Justifying switching back
I eventually switched back to NextCloud, and there were multiple reasons why.

Pros of OpenCloud
- Simple to set up
- Low resource footprint

Cons of OpenCloud
- Buggy uploads
- Slow uploads
- UI has a learning curve
- Oversimplified in the wrong ways

Pros of NextCloud
- Similar to Dropbox, Google Drive, and other public cloud services
- Lots of options
- Balance of advanced features and simplicity

Cons of NextCloud
- Had major bugs the last time I used it
- Very high resource footprint
- Has extra bloat

The pros of OpenCloud couldn't outweigh its cons, and NextCloud had already fixed all of those. So the cons of NextCloud were what I set out to fix.

The first issue, ***major bugs***, was quite easy to fix. Over time, NextCloud had been updated, and many of the bugs that were problematic before had been resolved, so things were looking great.

The next issue, ***high resource footprint***, was harder to tackle. The difficulty was that I'd been running the AIO instead of just the Drive portion. That included things like Photos, which came with mini AI models to analyze images; Tasks, which included a whole UI for a to-do list; and Talk, which included chatting, video calling, and file-sharing integration, all backed by a server handling those requests. After dropping everything and keeping only the Drive/Files portion of NextCloud, my footprint dropped significantly. I also moved this server over to the NAS to better manage my apps and free up headroom on the Linux box for my other servers. Removing the other parts of NextCloud also got rid of all the bloat, solving the ***extra bloat*** issue.

### How I switched back
Since all my files, for both me and my team, were on OpenCloud, I had to figure out a clean way to transfer everything over to NextCloud. A simple solution was rclone. It is a handy application that can connect to proprietary file systems, pull the data, and transfer it to a new location. This was useful since OpenCloud doesn't store files as a simple, normal directory structure. With this tool, my job was made easier and I was able to copy all my files over to NextCloud with ease. However, there were some minor hiccups, especially since this was a migration over the network. These were very small such as the network dropping and permission issues, but besides that, it was straightforward.

# Conclusion
The major change to my stack was replacing OpenCloud with NextCloud, for reasons listed above. This brought a significant improvement to the Cloud Drive problem.
