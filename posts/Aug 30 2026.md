# My First Post

I'll be explaining each part of my home lab, how they work together, and essentially what I have done so far. I have been working on this since May 2026. There are some major changes I have done to this through the months that I would like to share.
I have created 3 main services for BTcloud. These are:
- Talk
- Cloud
- AI

Those are the 3 most important services to me. I created BTcloud because I struggled to find a good source that is private, easy to use, and genuinely works that does the 3 main services I needed. After getting nowhere, I just decided to make it myself. The list that follows is some goals I wanted in this project. (this applies to all 3 services):
1. Private (no information goes near any big-tech service)
2. Local (all information is stored on my hardware)
3. Secure (ensure that everything done on BTcloud stays on BTcloud)

Here are some goals that are specific to each service:
> Talk: This has to be secure, e2ee, and have no/low amounts of limits on screensharing quality or anything that has to do with the speed of user's connections. User's hardware should always be the limit.

> Cloud Drive: This has to be easy to use, easy to share with people, and familiar. This means something like Google Drive or Microsoft OneDrive.

> AI: Instead of just making slop, I wanted to make AI useful in a way that could fit my 3 main goals. Making something similar to JARVIS is what many people think when running a local private AI model, but it's not the best idea in my opinion. I focused on making sure that tool usage and the UI itself were proper.

BTcloud first started off as a gimmick. I did not find it useful in any way; the texting server I chose was awful. However, I had created something, somewhere to start. From then on, I decided to make BTcloud mainly private, putting auth walls wherever possible and not advertising services that were private. These include my photos server and my Minecraft server.

### AI
My first iteration of BTcloud started with AI. I was heavily invested in creating LLMs and trying to build my own AI service that I can use to effectively replace ChatGPT and Claude for many general jobs and some advanced jobs. I started by using Ollama with some Gemma and Qwen models. At this stage, things were basic. You could chat with the model, get some information, but it was mostly basic information. Nothing was serious. I explored different models and somehow came to the conclusion that Gemma 4 models were the best. This was later proven very wrong. I made a full lineup of Gemma models with 1 Qwen-Coder model and started testing. I was able to make some basic HTML files that essentially had buttons to common websites such as Google, Wikipedia, Discord, etc. Whilst I was tinkering, I decided that the entire lineup deserved a name. After a few moments, I came up with ORION. Each letter stood for something like O standing for Operational and what not, but I mainly chose the name because I liked how it sounded and the fact that it was space related. In this ORION lineup, I had ORION-Fast, ORION-Think, and ORION-Code. These 3 are quite self-explanatory. One model was super fast at responding, the next took time to give the best response, and the last was tailored to coding. I gave each a system prompt and with the basic tool usage at the time provided with OpenWebUI, I continued my testing. This was around the time when I discovered the Qwen3 and Qwen 3.6 lineup in its entirety. After more testing, I came to the right conclusion that these Qwen models, although small, really pack a punch and can genuinely give good responses. I replaced the ORION lineup with the following: ORION-Fast, ORION-Think, ORION-Code. The fast model has Qwen3-8B, Think has Qwen3.6:27B, and the coder had a Qwen Coder model. This was around the time I discovered that OpenWebUI has advanced parameters for each model, allowing for specific fine tune controls. I also discovered that the defaults were terrible for Qwen models as there were specific settings that Alibaba (the producers of the Qwen lineup) recommended. After tinkering around with that bit, I was finally getting close to something that was good. Ollama as the backend, OpenWebUI as the front, it seemed fairly simple and performed well. I used this for a few weeks but then figured that these models just were not going to cut it for general activities, especially because of how small the context limit was and how slow the responses were, both due to my hardware. I then moved on to using OpenRouter and Groq. This was difficult for me because this would mean that my data and model completion would go online and potentially be seen by others. To combat this, in OpenRouter, I specifically used the option to block those that cache my responses. This gave me some peace of mind that at least not all of my data would be given up once sent. I still use it sparingly with OpenRouter, only asking questions that were not personal. This was an issue because to replace ChatGPT and Claude, I had to get specific, often that included saying things that I would not want to be used in training data. I then was introduced to llama.cpp and its fork, ik_llama.cpp, by a friend. He also introduced me to llama-swap. I decided to give it a shot and the first thing that happened after hours of setting up was more failures. I, at the time, couldn't figure out why the server wouldn't boot and run properly. After a week of trying different things, I figured out that my specific AMD card was old and didn't support ROCm. I was stuck with using a ROCm translational layer or Vulkan. I decided to go with the official route of Vulkan to ensure stability through updates. This is when I hit my first major roadblock. ik_llama does not support Vulkan very well as others do. I didn't know this, and for weeks I struggled to understand why I was performing worse in this new software that was supposed to be better than Ollama. I eventually figured out that passing a mainline server command used a Vulkan version that worked with my card. The performance boost I got was phenomenal. I went from 1-4 tokens/sec on an 8B model to almost 40 tokens/sec. This was big because now my models were working double that of Ollama. This was also around the time that I was introduced to MOE models. Essentially these models have a large set of knowledge and work similarly to that of a large model such as a 27B model, but only have 3B, or whatever the model is made to have, active. Qwen3 has an A3B (active 3B) model that I gave a shot at using. With this model, I was able to get around the intelligence of a 27B model at the speeds of an 8B model. This was due to the fact that I had low amounts of VRAM compared to normal RAM, which is where MOE models work the best. Putting the active params on the GPU allowed for fast responses while having the ability to choose across 30B params. With this, I expanded the ORION lineup to what I have now: 
- ORION (Local), which uses Qwen3-8B
- ORION-Email (Local), which uses Qwen3-8B
- ORION-Agent (Local), which uses Qwen3-30B-A3B
- ORION-Code (Local), which uses Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B
- ORION-Think (Local), which uses Qwen3.8-27B
- Backend, which uses Qwen3-0.6B for tasks such as conversation title generation and conversation compression
- ORION-Open (Local), which uses Qwen3.8-27B-Uncensored, a model that has no safeguards or restrictions
- ORION-Fast (Local), which uses Qwen3-8B
- ORION-Flash (Local), which uses Qwen3-0.6B

I also still use some models with OpenRouter as they have some good free models: 
- ORION-Code, which uses Groq.Qwen3.6-27B
- ORION-Fast, which uses OR.Minimax-M3
- ORION-Think, which uses OR.Nvidia Nemotron-3-Ultra-550B-A55B
- ORION-RandomModel, which uses the OpenRouter free random model router. 

With all of these models, I was finally able to safely leave behind ChatGPT. I am still working on finding a good replacement for Claude, as their Claude Code CLI is amazing and theoretically can hook into LLMs. As of right now, I have not created a personal replacement for Claude and I continue to use it. Many of the skills that Claude uses are downloaded onto my server for the LLMs to use. This wraps up the AI section of my home lab.
